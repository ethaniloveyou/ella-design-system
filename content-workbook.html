🗣️ Your voice — keep this DNA

  • Plainspoken. Write like you’d talk to one woman across a table. Short sentences.
  • Second person. “You”, not “women” or “patients”, wherever you can.
  • No alarmism, no judgment. Calm and matter-of-fact beats scary.
  • Options, not endorsements. Explain choices; don’t prescribe or sell.
  • Specific over vague. A concrete example beats an adjective.
  • No em-dashes (your preference). Commas or short sentences instead.

📚 Sources & citations — set this up now

For a health site, an unsourced statistic is a liability. Decide a simple, repeatable pattern. My suggestion:

  • End each article with a “Sources” list of 2–5 links. In the text, a claim can read naturally (“research suggests…”) and link out.
  • Lean on authorities, not random blogs: The Menopause Society (NAMS), ACOG, Office on Women’s Health, NICE, PubMed.
  • Claims already on the site that need a source (find or soften each):
• “About half of all women go through menopause” • “Most medical curricula spend less than 1 hour on menopause” • “Perimenopause lasts 7–14 years” • “We lose ~1% of muscle per year after ~45” • “Aim for 1.2–1.6 g protein per kg of body weight”
